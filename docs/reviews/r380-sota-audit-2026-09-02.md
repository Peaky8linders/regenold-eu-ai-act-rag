# R380 — end-to-end audit after the 2026-08-25 report: KG, ontology, retrieval stack, Stage-2 prompt, hard mode

Date 2026-09-02. Base `dc50bd3` (R379). Branch `r380-sota-conciseness`.

## 0. What the report says, and the arithmetic that decides the roadmap

The official report (110 questions, easy + hard, geometric mean of 8 axes) has us at
75.1 easy / 73.4 hard against a 2026 frontier baseline of 80.9 / 81.7. Every axis except
the two conciseness axes improved since July; Answer Conciseness fell 96.0 → 51.9 (easy)
and 93.4 → 45.2 (hard), Reference Conciseness 79.3 → 50.4 / 72.1 → 49.8. Because Overall
is a geometric mean, those two collapses ate the whole correctness gain.

Marginal leverage of one point on each axis (pp of Overall per pp), computed from the
report's own numbers: AnsCon **0.179** (easy) / **0.201** (hard), RefCon **0.185 / 0.183**,
every other axis 0.094–0.137. The pairs that beat the frontier baseline holding all else
fixed: easy needs roughly (AnsCon 70, RefCon 70) or (75, 65) or (80, 60); hard needs
(80, 70) or (85, 65) or (90, 60).

Calibration that changes what "conciseness" means here: the July answers that scored 96
averaged **915 chars, 4.2 sentences, 3.99 raw refs** (`jul07_answer` in the official
batch snapshot). The reference answers in our probe set average 228 chars, one sentence.
So the official axis is an LLM-judged *"more verbose than necessary relative to the
reference answer"* on CONTENT, not a length ratio: a 900-char answer that stays on the
question scores high; a 1,200-char answer that appends adjacent law scores half. Length is
a screening proxy only.

## 1. Audits (five read-only agents plus a live hard-mode probe)

### 1.1 Stage-2 user message (the only channel the wrapper delivers)

Measured by capturing the assembled user message offline:

| block | chars |
| :--- | ---: |
| EU AI ACT REFERENCES (verbatim provisions) | 9,800 – 30,400 |
| CROSS-REFERENCED PROVISIONS ("background only") | 500 – 1,500 |
| Stage-1 draft | 1,500 – 2,500 (hard mode 5,500) |
| instruction clauses (V2 stack) | ~11,700 |

Root causes of the unasked trailing sentences, all default ON:

1. The live path is fully uncapped (`REGENOLD_ANSWER_NO_CAP=1`,
   `REGENOLD_LIVE_SENTENCE_CAP=0` at `app/integrations/regenold/models.py:1466`); the
   offline path keeps `MAX_ANSWER_SENTENCES=3`, which is why the offline July-vs-HEAD
   replay was byte-identical while the live answers grew.
2. The length rule ("AT MOST 4 sentences") sits on the SYSTEM channel, delivered on 0% of
   requests; the user channel's only length line is followed by an escape hatch
   ("use additional sentences for another risk tier, a carve-out, or a cross-reference,
   or when rule 12b …") whose "rule 12b" points into the undelivered system prompt.
3. Three clauses explicitly instruct adjacent law: "state both the prohibited context AND
   its treatment elsewhere"; "when one provision depends on another, name both and what
   each contributes"; the cross-referenced-provisions block.
4. The deterministic draft already carries the adjacent rosters (the eight Annex III
   areas on an Article 7 question, the Article 6(3) derogation on a definitional one,
   Article 38 on an Article 50 one) and the instruction is "Refine the draft".
5. Hard mode: `_MAX_QUESTION_CHARS = 64_000` lets all nine history turns into the
   retrieval query when the de-noiser does not fire; REFERENCES 9.8k → 30.2k, draft
   2.5k → 5.5k, wire refs 1 → 5 on the probed question.

What shipped: `REGENOLD_PROMPT_V3` (default OFF pending the gate) — one 6k-char block
appended last, replacing the 11.7k stack. `tests/test_r380_prompt_v3.py` pins it on the
wire, two-sided.

### 1.2 Hard mode, live (six official questions, easy / turn-1 / pushback, traced)

* The Stage-0 de-noiser returned `fallback_reason: truncated` on **5 of 9** multi-turn
  calls: `max_tokens=100` against `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, a reasoning model whose hidden
  reasoning counts against the budget. Every provider in the chain truncated the same
  way, so the caller fell through to the 40-turn concatenation. Turn-1 answers gained
  history provisions (Article 26, Article 49, Annex I) and one question about the
  Article 111 transitional rule shipped `['Article 6', 'Article 5']`.
* The R305 re-ask focus, checked against the evaluator's VERBATIM pushback template,
  fires on **100 of 110** official questions; the ten misses are anchor-less questions
  ("Who is entitled to lodge a complaint …"), which then take the truncating de-noiser
  path with the disputed answer and the pushback text in the query. Pushback answers on
  that path measured 1.2x–2.3x the easy-mode length.
* At `0033b88` the pushback answers started with a literal `<reasoning_scratchpad>.`
  (R377's tag instruction without the extractor); R379 (`dc50bd3`) ports the extractor.

What shipped: `REGENOLD_DENOISER_MAX_TOKENS` (default 400) and `REGENOLD_REASK_ANCHORLESS`
(default ON): 110/110 official pushbacks now re-ask their own question.
`tests/test_r380_hard_mode_reask.py`.

A side effect surfaced by the paired hard-mode run (§ 2.2): with a budget the rewrite can
actually spend, the Stage-0 model returns a PARAPHRASE of a question that already stood
alone, and the paraphrase misses the curated intercepts and exact-match paths the verbatim
question hits (two of fourteen rows went from a deterministic `Annex III.8` answer to a
Stage-2 answer citing Article 6). `REGENOLD_DENOISE_SELF_CONTAINED_SKIP` (default ON)
therefore returns a self-contained live turn verbatim before any provider is dialled: hard
turn 1 is now identical to easy mode for the 100/110 self-contained official questions, and
the 0.5–3 s rewrite leaves the critical path. Live-only by construction (the branch sits
after the no-provider exit), so the cli bench is byte-identical. Measured in § 2.4.

Because that predicate now decides the PRIMARY path, its false positives matter more than
they did for the provider-failure salvage: `_live_turn_is_self_contained` therefore also
rejects a turn that opens with a conjunction ("And the reporting duties for those
providers?", "But we built it, so are we provider or deployer?"). Rejects 0/110 official
questions and the one conjunction-led follow-up among the 37 multi-turn probe rows.

### 1.4 Anchor maps and ontology

Seven provisions had ZERO engine anchors (`_KEYWORD_ENTITY_MAP`), the R367 Article 7
class: Article 17 (gold-cited), Annex IV (gold-cited), Article 12, Annex IX, Annex X,
and Articles 97 / 98 (zero in BOTH maps). Fixed with provision-specific phrases;
"annex ix" / "annex x" word-bounded; 0 davidath hits on all 476 questions except one
Article 12 question that the anchor makes more right. `tests/test_r380_anchor_gaps.py`.

A second anchor gap surfaced by the multi-turn gate rows: emotion recognition described
by its INPUT ("reads employees' facial expressions during the meeting to score their
engagement") reached neither map nor the prohibited gatekeeper, so offline the turn was
answered with the FRIA roster (history bleed) or refused (no scope anchor), and live the
V3 arm called it limited-risk. "facial expression" / "emotional state" / "infer emotions"
now anchor Art. 5 (+ Art. 50) in the engine map and the scope map, "facial expression" joins the AI-marker-gated sensitive-attribute anchors (R112 principle: the bare phrase stays out of scope),
and the gatekeeper's verb-object pattern accepts "facial expressions" as the
emotional-state noun. 0 davidath hits.

Legally wrong bindings, recorded and deliberately NOT changed (the table is a reference
GENERATOR measured at 0% precision as a citation oracle, and removing a binding drops
refs on questions whose gold cites the right): `ontology.py` binds Art. 13 to DEPLOYER
(Art. 13(1) binds the provider) and lists Art. 85/86 as AFFECTED_PERSON obligations (they
are rights); `role_obligations.py` binds Art. 72 to DEPLOYER (Art. 72(1) binds the
provider); DOWNSTREAM_PROVIDER lists Art. 53/55 (they bind the upstream model provider).
Annex summaries: all thirteen verified against the official titles.

### 1.5 Retrieval stack

`REGENOLD_GRAPH_SEMANTIC_LAYERS`, `REGENOLD_GRAPH_VECTOR_RECALL` and `REGENOLD_COHERE_RERANK`
all FIRE live when switched on (counters moved; Neo4j vector queries in the driver log);
all default OFF and unmeasured. The default-ON dense fill (`REGENOLD_TURBOQUANT_DENSE`)
flips silently between Cohere `embed-english-v3.0` and local SVD on a 429, so retrieval is
nondeterministic under Cohere rate limits (observed twice today). The candidate set
freezes at `references = candidates[:_effective_max_refs]` before the three prose→refs
passes reopen the wire list after Stage-2.

### 1.6 MUVERA

At ~1,800 provisions, exact Chamfer/MaxSim is a single matmul (≈1.4 GFLOP, tens of ms).
Fixed Dimensional Encodings only approximate the same score faster, so they cannot touch
the failure class that matters here (semantically plausible, legally inapposite
references). No keyed provider returns token-level vectors without torch (Jina v4 is the
one hosted option). Do not build FDE. The cheap variant worth a probe — one vector per
paragraph/point, score a provision by max-sim over its set — must move the wrong-reference
rate on the known-distractor rows with `gold_dropped_head` flat before it goes near the gate.

### 1.7 The Bedrock trap, measured

The wrapper shares the operator session's Claude Max quota. When this session hit its
limit, a 40-row paired screen read `primary_ok 14 / fallback_ok 66`: 66 of 80 Stage-2
calls were silently Bedrock. The runner now attributes every row to `wrapper | bedrock |
failed` from `stage2_policy.transport_stats()` and aborts on the first fallback row.
