## Executive summary

The round's engineering is sound and its gates are real — I reproduced all four
independently, digit for digit. But the review found **three Critical defects, all
in the hard-rule-#4 class (a confidently-wrong legal statement), and all live on
`main`**, plus a narrative overstatement in the round's headline claim.

The sharpest is a compounding chain the round did not see because one of its own
Cypher alternations is dead: **Article 5(1) reaches the model at 19% of its length,
with prohibitions (c) through (h) absent — while R323's new sub-point block injects
the (h) real-time-RBI *exceptions* whose parent prohibition is missing.** The block's
own heading says "a prohibition stated without its exception is WRONG"; the shipped
combination is the inverse of that.

Nothing here argues for reverting the round. R323 is a net improvement on every
axis it touched. The findings are what it left open or propagated.

## Critical

### [C1] The foreign-citation guard has three live holes; R323 closed one of four shapes
- **File:** `app/routes/regenold.py:3776` (widened), `:3830` (not widened), `:3872` (56-char window), `:3880` (24-char window)
- **Bug:** R323 widened `_NUMBERED_REG_RE` to accept the `No ` particle and 3-digit
  sequence numbers — correctly, for the plain ahead form. Three sibling shapes remain
  open: (a) the *prefix* form, because `_FOREIGN_INSTRUMENT_BEHIND_RE` still carries
  the pre-R323 narrow `\d{4}/\d+`; (b) any ahead form with an interposed clause, because
  the 56-char window truncates before the regulation number — and R323's own `No `
  particle costs 3 more characters, pushing *more* citations past the boundary; (c) the
  bare form without the `(EU)` parenthetical.
- **Reproduced end-to-end** through `_add_prose_named_refs` on HEAD:

  | prose | shipped |
  |---|---|
  | `Article 30 of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008` | `[]` — R323's fix works |
  | `Article 10, second subparagraph, point (b), of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012` | **`Article 10`** |
  | `Article 6(1), point (a), second subparagraph, of Regulation (EU) 2016/679` | **`Article 6`** |
  | `Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, Article 10 requires standards.` | **`Article 10`** |
  | `Under Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Article 35 requires a DPIA.` | **`Article 35`** |
  | *control:* `Article 13 requires transparency…` | `Article 13` ✓ |

- **Impact:** GDPR Art. 35 (DPIA) ships as AI Act Article 35 (notified-body
  identification numbers); GDPR Art. 6 (lawful basis) as AI Act Article 6 (high-risk
  classification). Every fabricated number resolves in `ARTICLE_EXISTENCE`, so the
  hard-rule-#5 lint structurally cannot catch them — the same argument R323's own
  commit message makes for its fix.
- **Suggested fix:** extract the numbering fragment to one module-level constant used
  by both guards; bound the ahead window at the next sentence terminator or next
  `Article N` mention (whichever is first) *then* widen it. ⚠ Do **not** simply widen
  the window — demonstrated: at 120 chars, `"Article 13 requires transparency for
  high-risk systems. Article 35 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 requires a DPIA."` drops
  the genuine Article 13.
- **Confidence:** High (reproduced by me) · **Found by:** Logic, Contract, Security, Error-handling (4/5 lanes)

### [C2] Article 5(1) reaches the model at 19%, with its exceptions injected but its prohibitions cut
- **File:** `app/engines/kg_context.py:164` (dead alternation), `:221-231` (`_flat` cliff)
- **Bug:** two defects compounding.
  1. `OPTIONAL MATCH (a)-[:HAS_PARAGRAPH|HAS_POINT]->(u)` binds `a` to Article/Annex,
     but **all 421 `HAS_POINT` edges have a Paragraph source** (measured). The
     alternation reaches **0 of 421** Point nodes — it is dead, and it is missing from
     the round's own inventory of unread layers precisely because the query *looks*
     like it covers Point.
  2. `_flat`'s enumeration guard promises in its docstring that an enumerated unit is
     "delivered whole up to `_UNIT_HARD_CEILING`; beyond that explicitly marked
     truncated". The code instead falls back to `limit` (default 900, max 1200), so
     `_UNIT_HARD_CEILING` is a switch, not a ceiling.
- **Measured:** Article 5(1) is 4701 chars; `_flat(t, 900)` returns **905 chars (19%)**,
  cut mid-point-(b) at *"…their age, disability or a specific social or economic
  situation, with the objective, [...]"*. Points (c) social scoring, (d) predictive
  policing, (e) facial scraping, (f) workplace emotion recognition, (g) biometric
  categorisation, (h) real-time RBI never reach the prompt. Seven units hit this cliff:
  `article_5_1` (4701), `annex_VII_4` (4520), `article_66_1` (4507), `article_60_4`
  (4479), `article_59_1` (3320), `article_58_2` (2938), `annex_IV_2` (2736) — the last
  being technical documentation, named in the round's own comments.
- **Impact:** hard rule #4. R323's new `_SUBPOINT_CYPHER` injects
  `article_5_1_h_i/ii/iii` — the RBI carve-outs — into a prompt whose Article 5(1)
  prohibition text stops at (b). No env value fixes it: `REGENOLD_KG_UNIT_CHARS`
  clamps at 1200 and the ceiling is a module constant.
- **Suggested fix:** fix the query first — `MATCH (a)-[:HAS_PARAGRAPH]->(p)` then
  `OPTIONAL MATCH (p)-[:HAS_POINT]->(pt)`, emitting points as their own units. Each
  point is then short enough to survive `_flat` and the cliff stops mattering. As a
  stopgap, make the enum guard set `limit = _UNIT_HARD_CEILING` and fall through to the
  boundary search rather than returning the 900-char cut.
- **Confidence:** High (both halves reproduced by me) · **Found by:** Error-handling (95/90)

### [C3] The char budget deletes the two blocks R323 exists to add, at ordinary ref counts
- **File:** `app/engines/kg_context.py:509-539` (`used`), `:687-690` (tail-drop)
- **Bug:** `used` accumulates **only** provision-structure lines and is budgeted against
  the whole `max_chars`, so that one block may consume the entire ceiling. The sub-point
  and deontic blocks are then appended with no budget participation at all, and the tail
  loop `while len(parts) > 1 and sum(...) > max_chars: parts.pop()` removes them first.
- **Measured** against the real corpus at the default 16000:

  | refs | blocks kept | sub-point block |
  |---|---|---|
  | Art 5, 6 | 4 | kept |
  | Art 5, 6, 50 | 4 | kept |
  | Art 6, 9, 10, 11 | 4 | kept |
  | **Art 9–15 (HRAIS chain)** | **1** | **dropped** |
  | **8 refs (`_DEFAULT_MAX_REFS`)** | **1** | **dropped** |

  Per-article render is median 2008 / p90 4482 chars, so the cliff sits at ~4–5 refs —
  ordinary, not the "pathological tail" the comment claims. At the module's own designed
  maximum the new layers are entirely off.
- **Impact:** the feature is closest to being deleted on exactly the multi-provision
  requests it was built for. Compounds C2: cite Article 5 in a chain and you keep the
  truncated prohibition and lose the carve-outs.
- **Suggested fix:** reserve budget before spending it (cap the provision loop at
  ~0.6·`max_chars`, `used` already tracks it), or trim provision *units* in the tail loop
  rather than popping whole downstream blocks. Also: `used += len(head)` runs *after* the
  admission decisions, so heads are excluded from the fit test; and `dropped_over_cap`
  conflates units-dropped with blocks-dropped in one trace note.
- **Confidence:** High · **Found by:** Security (90), Logic (88), Error-handling (72), Contract (72) — 4/5 lanes

### [I2] Annex VIII Sections B and C became unreachable — and the new API that fixes it is inert
`app/data/provision_text.py:199, 253-268`. The new `setdefault` first-section-wins collapse is
the right doctrine for a bare `Annex VIII(1)`, but `annex_items_sectioned` has **no production
consumer** (only `_annex_items` and its test), so 16 items have no addressable citation at all:
Annex VIII 27 sectioned → 13 flat (14 lost), Annex XI 5 → 3 (2 lost). Measured reachable text

*(Contract 85, Logic 82, Security 80, Error-handling 78 — 4/5 lanes)*

### [I3] Article 3's units are sorted lexicographically — 44 of 68 definitions dropped
`app/engines/kg_context.py:159-166` (`ORDER BY a.id, u.number`) with `number` a **string**
property. At `_DEFAULT_MAX_UNITS = 24` the kept set is `1, 10–19, 2, 20–29, 3, 30` and the
dropped set is `4,5,6,7,8,9, 31–68`. Verified by hand. So whenever Article 3 is cited, 3(4)
**deployer**, 3(5) authorised representative, 3(6) importer, 3(7) distributor are silently
absent while 3(10)–3(30) survive. Pre-existing in `_HIERARCHY_CYPHER`; R323's new
`_SUBPOINT_CYPHER` copies the pattern. Fix: `toInteger(u.number)` with a coalesce guard for
non-numeric annex labels. *(Security, 85)*

### [I4] `kg_context` bypasses the wall-clock budget and circuit breaker that R323 itself re-tuned
`app/engines/kg_context.py:307-320, 252, 355, 428`. Five `client.execute_read` calls go direct;
the module imports nothing from `app.graph.timeouts`. Every other graph consumer
(`graph_expand_2hop`, `graph_aware_retrieval`, `_graph_rag_impl`) wraps the read in
`graph_circuit_open()` + `resolve_graph_timeout_ms()` + `record_graph_{success,failure}()`.
Against a *hung* host one read costs `3 × 5.0s + 1.5s ≈ 16.5s` (R321's measured figure for the
same call shape). The breaker is bypassed in both directions: it cannot shed these calls, and
their failures never help open it. This is the round's sharp irony — effort spent raising
`DEFAULT_GRAPH_TIMEOUT_MS` 500→750 while adding two queries the budget does not cover. Partly
offset by the new memo (renders 3→1, so reads 9→5). *(Security 85, Error-handling 82, Logic 70)*

### [I5] `SEED_VERSION` is written before the fail-soft hierarchy ingest — a partial graph marks itself current
`scripts/seed_neo4j_kb.py:126, 1243, 1670`. `seed_graph()` writes `KBMetadata.seed_version`, then
`_ingest_legal_ast_hierarchy` runs — fail-soft, catching and returning 0, while `execute_write_batch`
raises. A mid-run blip aborts the remaining batches with `seed_version` already saying "current",
so every later boot hits `skip-current` permanently. The missing layer is precisely the one
`_SUBPOINT_CYPHER` and the provision-structure block depend on, and nothing surfaces it
(`kg_context` logs at debug; `/healthz/graph` reports raw counts with no expected value to compare).
Fix: write `KBMetadata` last, or record `hierarchy_node_count` on it and compare at boot.
*(Error-handling, 75)*

### [I6] `validate_payload` never inspects the hierarchy — the exact bucket R323 mutates
`scripts/seed_neo4j_kb.py:1337-1384`. `node_ids` is built from 14 buckets, none of them
`paragraph_nodes`/`point_nodes`/`subpoint_nodes`; `edge_buckets` omits
`HAS_PARAGRAPH`/`HAS_POINT`/`HAS_SUBPOINT`. Measured: `validate_payload(build_payload())` returns
0 errors while never touching **1116 hierarchy nodes and 1116 hierarchy edges** — a dangling
`HAS_POINT` cannot be caught. R323 is the round that changes these counts (656→658 Paragraph,
416→421 Point) and bumps `SEED_VERSION` to advertise it. Note `counts()` is *fine* — its omission
is deliberate and documented; this is `validate_payload` only. CLAUDE.md's R321 line *"validate_payload()
now lints the new edges too"* is stale for hierarchy edges. *(Contract, 88)*

### [I7] `_RENDER_MEMO`'s docstring names a per-request reset that does not exist
`app/engines/kg_context.py:454-470` asserts *"reset per request by the route's trace setup"*.
Nothing in `app/` resets it; only the tests do. The route resets `_CURRENT` (a different ContextVar,
in a different module, and only when `?include_reasoning=true`) and `_request_intent_cache`. Isolation
holds today only because the endpoint is a sync `def`, so anyio runs each call in a fresh
`copy_context()` — verified empirically (`ThreadPoolExecutor` reuse leaks; `anyio.to_thread` does not).
The false claim is load-bearing: it is the stated reason a ContextVar is safe where an `lru_cache`
would not be. Making the endpoint `async def`, or moving prompt assembly behind one of the three
module-level persistent executors, converts it to a per-worker process-lifetime cache — and it
memoizes an empty render, so a transient graph failure pins `[]`. Correct precedent sits four lines
away: `_request_intent_cache.set({})`, reset unconditionally. *(Contract 90, Security 75, Error-handling 68, Logic 66)*

## Narrative corrections

These are not code defects. They are claims in the commit messages, `CLAUDE.md`, or
`.planning/R323-HANDOFF.md` that a future session would otherwise inherit as fact.

1. **The W2 headline is overstated.** "The graph blocks were silently disarming the
   citation-drift guard" — they were not, because `grounded_refs` never feeds a drop
   decision. I read both consumers: `:6663` is a non-emptiness test, and at `:6825/:6833`
   the drop path is purely `ref not in ARTICLE_EXISTENCE`, with `grounded_refs` only
   deciding whether a ref is appended to `tolerated` → a `stage2_ungrounded_cite_tolerated`
   trace note. R113 deliberately made ungrounded-but-real refs *tolerated*. The change is
   **telemetry-only**. Two upsides: it carries zero gold-drop risk (so hard rule #8 is not
   engaged), and the threading is genuinely correct — `include_grounding=False` reaches
   exactly one call site, guarded by a source-scan test. But the entry as written will lead
   a future round to believe a guard is armed that is not, on the standing open problem
   (over-citation). *(I asserted the mechanism was sound earlier in this review on the basis
   of the threading alone; that was incomplete — the threading is right, the claim about
   what it achieves is not.)*
2. **`.evalout/celex_verified.json` is lost, not ready.** The handoff lists it as available
   groundwork ("67/67 CELEX empirically verified"). `.evalout/` is gitignored, the file was
   never committed, and it is absent from this checkout — so it is unrecoverable from git.
   Treat the foreign-instrument verification as work to redo (by probing the Cellar, not
   heuristically), not as a ready artifact.
3. **"The vector layer is dead" is the wrong wording.** The grep claim is correct — nothing
   queries the 7 Neo4j VECTOR indexes, and the same is true of the `ft_provision_prose`
   full-text index nobody mentioned. But two *other* dense paths are default-ON and firing
   on every retrieval: `embeddings_index` (`REGENOLD_EMBEDDINGS_INDEX` default `"1"`) and
   `turboquant_index` (default `"1"`), both wired into `kb_search.top_articles_by_relevance`.
   The genuinely dead in-process path is `vector_rerank` (env-OFF *and* assets absent).
   Accurate framing: "the Neo4j vector and full-text indexes are unconsumed."
4. **CLAUDE.md lines ~1508 and ~1606 are stale — confirmed.** `kb_search.py:1055` imports
   `_build_xref_graph`, not `_build_xref_graph_core`. Measured impact: 47 of 77 articles'
   boost values differ between core and full, and 19 are lifted from no-boost into the boost
   tier. Important nuance the handoff omits: R57-C made this change **deliberately**, said so
   in the docstring, and paid for it by lowering the boost cap 1.15 → 1.10 in the same round.
   So the CLAUDE.md sentence is stale; it is not an unnoticed regression.
5. **The `REGENOLD_KG_MAX_CHARS` reasoning is wrong even though the default is right.** The
   handoff argues a 4000 cap is unsafe because "Article 5's *first* unit alone exceeds it".
   It does not: units are pre-truncated by `_flat` and render at 905 chars, no unit can exceed
   2600, and the first unit is admitted unconditionally. The conclusion still holds on other
   grounds (typical renders are 5–14k), but not for that reason.
6. **`SEED_VERSION` advertises sections the graph does not have** (see I2). Consider
   `r323-annex-first-section-wins`.
7. **`app/graph/embedded_graph.py:371, 378-383` state that the unset default backend is
   `embedded`.** It is `neo4j` (`:325`, flipped by R313.1). I trusted those docstrings and
   put the wrong default in an earlier draft of this review, so they have now cost at least
   one reader — worth correcting in place.
