## 3. The reading this changes

- **The gate.** Under the pre-R461 definition the count-only arm is
  VETO on `rg_037`; under the lever definition it is
  **CLEAN**, because that row never ran the block:
  arm B shipped the deterministic Stage-1 draft, and the row's 7 refs /
  1,474 chars against a stated budget of 2 are what a failed Stage-2 leg
  looks like. On the 27 rows the block actually
  served, the count-only arm drops no gold head at all.
- **R460's own veto, re-read.** The full block's rule-#8 refusal rested
  on the same row id, `rg_037` (B=prior_turn): the truncation guard kept the previous
  turn's answer, so the block never served that row. That refusal now
  reads **CLEAN** — which does NOT promote the full
  block: R460 failed its other targets (ans_conciseness -4.49 pp,
  answers +64 chars) and every one of those is untouched by this change.
  What the re-read corrects is the reason given for the refusal, which
  is the part of the record the next reader would otherwise trust.

## 4. What the fix must NOT do, and does not

- **The noise floor still vetoes.** NOISE FLOOR  (byte-identical prompts): two draws of an
  UNCHANGED arm on byte-identical prompts — `rg_061`, `rg_088`
  — both `primary`/`primary`, so the scope keeps them in and the verdict
  stays **VETO**. A single-draw gate still cannot decide rule #8 at
  n=37; that finding is unchanged and unaffected by where the veto is read.
- **The measured deltas are untouched.** `ref_conciseness` on the gate pair
  reads +7.69 [+1.41, +15.05]
  under both scopes, and so does every other axis (asserted).
- **Unreadable provenance does not lift a veto.** With no checkpoint to
  read, the scope falls back to `all` and says so; a row whose provenance
  is missing or names nothing is reported UNDECIDED, which is not CLEAN.
