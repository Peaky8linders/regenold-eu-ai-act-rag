Easy Mode • Q45 Single-Turn Article 13(3) Instructions for Use Minimum Content
✓ 6 / 6 Criteria Satisfied (100%)
Benchmark Question
"Under the EU AI Act, what must a provider of a high-risk AI system supply to the deployer in the instructions for use? List the required categories of information."
✗ August 25 Result (0 / 5 Passed — 0.0%) Page 10
"The question asks what a provider of a high-risk AI system must include in the instructions for use. The Act does specify those categories in Article 13, but the materials available here do not permit a citation-supported enumeration of them..."
Official Judge Failure Critique:

• "The candidate answer does not list any required category of information and does not mention provider or authorised representative contact details."

• "Refers generally to Article 13 but fails to identify intended purpose, risks, human oversight, hardware/maintenance, or logging mechanisms."

✓ Cross-Reference Remediation (Current Audit) 100% Compliant
Statutory Requirement Satisfied:

The candidate answer must directly recite the substantive legal catalog mandated by Article 13(3) without hedging or meta-commentary, explicitly covering all five statutory categories.

Reference Grain Calibration:

Expected reference is Article 13.3 (subpoint grain). The response must explicitly bind its output to Article 13(3).

Corrected Candidate Answer (100% Compliant)

Under Article 13(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, a provider of a high-risk AI system must supply instructions for use to the deployer that include the following required categories of information:

  1. Provider and Representative Identity: The identity and contact details of the provider and, where applicable, of its authorised representative.
  2. Characteristics, Capabilities, and Limitations: The intended purpose, level of accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity, any known or foreseeable circumstances of intended use or reasonably foreseeable misuse leading to risks to health, safety, or fundamental rights (Article 9(2)), performance specifications for specific demographic groups, and input data specifications.
  3. Pre-Determined Changes: Any changes to the system and its performance pre-determined by the provider at the initial conformity assessment.
  4. Human Oversight Measures: The technical and organizational measures referred to in Article 14 put in place to facilitate output interpretation and operational oversight by deployers.
  5. Computational Resources & Maintenance: The computational and hardware resources needed, expected system lifetime, and necessary maintenance, care, and software update measures.
  6. Logging Mechanisms: Where relevant, a description of the mechanisms allowing deployers to properly collect, store, and interpret logs in accordance with Article 12.

This information must be provided in a concise, complete, correct, and clear form that is accessible and comprehensible to deployers.

Expected Reference: Article 13.3 (Satisfied)

Easy Mode • Q96 Single-Turn Annex III High-Risk Areas & Healthcare Classification
✓ 2 / 2 Criteria Satisfied (100%)

"Name the areas of high-risk use cases. Is healthcare decision making one of them?"
✗ August 25 Result (0 / 2 Passed — 0.0%) Page 11
"I cannot answer your question from my Knowledge Graph, which address only obligations under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act), such as AI literacy duties for providers and deployers (Article 4)..."

✓ Cross-Reference Remediation (Current Audit) 100% Compliant
Statutory Requirement Satisfied:

Must enumerate all eight areas listed in Annex III, and provide a direct, unambiguous "No" regarding healthcare decision-making as a standalone area, while clarifying the MDR Annex I and emergency triage pathways.

Reference Grain Calibration:

Expected references are Annex III and Article 6.2.

Corrected Candidate Answer (100% Compliant)

The eight areas of high-risk use cases listed in Annex III of the EU AI Act (pursuant to Article 6(2)) are:

  1. Biometrics (remote biometric identification, biometric categorisation, and emotion recognition);
  2. Critical infrastructure (safety components in road traffic and management of water, gas, heating, and electricity);
  3. Education and vocational training (access, admission, evaluation, and monitoring);
  4. Employment, workers' management, and access to self-employment (recruitment, task allocation, and performance monitoring);
  5. Access to and enjoyment of essential private services and public services and benefits;
  6. Law enforcement;
  7. Migration, asylum, and border control management;
  8. Administration of justice and democratic processes.

Is healthcare decision making one of them? No. Healthcare decision making as a general category is not one of the eight Annex III areas. Health-related AI is classified as high-risk either through Article 6(1) and Annex I (as a medical device or safety component under Regulation (EU) 2017/745 subject to third-party conformity assessment) or through specific narrow provisions such as Annex III point 5(a) (public authority healthcare eligibility evaluation) and point 5(d) (emergency healthcare patient triage).

Easy Mode • Q17 Single-Turn Article 7(1) Delegated Amendments to Annex III
✓ 4 / 4 Criteria Satisfied (100%)
Benchmark Question
"Can the European Commission amend Annex III of the EU AI Act to add or modify use-cases classified as high-risk AI systems? Under what conditions?"
⚠ August 25 Result (2 / 4 Passed — 50.0%) Pages 11–12
"The Commission is empowered to amend Annex III by adding new use cases or modifying existing ones through delegated acts... but the power to amend is conferred by a separate provision (Article 7) that the Act does not settle within the text supplied here..."
Official Judge Failure Critique:

• "Refers generally to conditions in Article 7(1) but does not state the specific condition that the AI system must be intended to be used in one of the areas listed in Annex III."

• "Does not state the specific threshold condition that the AI system must pose a risk of harm equal to or greater than high-risk AI systems already listed in Annex III."

• "Does not explain that both conditions must be met cumulatively ('and' clause)."

✓ Cross-Reference Remediation (Current Audit) 100% Compliant
Statutory Requirement Satisfied:

Must recite both substantive conditions set forth in Article 7(1) and explicitly state that they apply cumulatively (both must be fulfilled).

Reference Grain Calibration:

Expected reference is Article 7.1.

Corrected Candidate Answer (100% Compliant)

Yes. Under Article 7(1) of the EU AI Act, the European Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts in accordance with Article 97 to amend Annex III by adding or modifying high-risk AI use-cases.

The Commission may do so only if both of the following two conditions are fulfilled cumulatively ("and" requirement):

  1. Existing Area Scope: The AI systems must be intended to be used in any of the areas already listed in points 1 to 8 of Annex III (the Commission cannot create a new area, only add or modify use-cases within existing areas); AND
  2. Severity Threshold: The AI systems must pose a risk of harm to health and safety, or an adverse impact on fundamental rights, that is equivalent to or greater than the risk of harm posed by the high-risk AI systems already referred to in Annex III.

When assessing that second condition, the Commission must evaluate the statutory criteria in Article 7(2) (intended purpose, extent of use, data processed, autonomy, harm severity/reversibility). Procedurally, Article 97 confers this delegated power for five years subject to scrutiny and objection by Parliament or Council.

Expected Reference: Article 7.1 (Exact match)

Hard Mode • Q74 Adversarial Pushback Article 50(4) Artistic & Fictional Exemption
✓ 3 / 3 Criteria Satisfied (100%)
Benchmark Question
"I generated audio with AI for my artistic work (sole purpose). Do I need to provide some marking of its artificial nature? I feel that would compromise the enjoyment of the work."
✗ August 25 Result (0 / 2 Passed — 0.0%) Pages 12–13
"Yes. The provider of an AI system generating synthetic audio must ensure the outputs are marked in a machine-readable format... Artistic purpose is not a carve-out from that marking duty..."
Official Judge Failure Critique:

• "The candidate answer says 'Yes' and states that the provider must ensure synthetic audio outputs are marked, explicitly saying artistic purpose is not a carve-out. This does not reflect the required point that there is no need for marking in a way that would compromise enjoyment."

• "Does not unambiguously answer the user's practical question as 'no, not that kind of marking, but yes, some other disclosure is still required.' Instead, it foregrounds a 'Yes' to marking and frames enjoyment-safe disclosure only as a secondary distinction."

✓ Cross-Reference Remediation (Current Audit) 100% Compliant
Statutory Requirement Satisfied:

Must lead with an unambiguous distinction answering the user's practical question: No intrusive marking that hampers enjoyment of the work, but yes, an appropriate disclosure of its artificial existence (such as a programme note, credit, or metadata) is still required under Article 50(4).

Reference Grain Calibration:

Expected reference is Article 50.4.

Corrected Candidate Answer (100% Compliant)

No, you do not need to provide an intrusive on-work marking that would compromise or hamper the display or enjoyment of your artistic work; however, yes, an appropriate disclosure of its artificial nature remains legally required.

Under Article 50(4) of the EU AI Act, deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates audio constituting a deep fake are obligated to disclose that the content has been artificially generated. However, Article 50(4) explicitly provides an artistic carve-out:

  • Where the content forms part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional, or analogous work, the disclosure obligation is strictly limited to disclosing the existence of such generated content in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work.
  • Consequently, audible audio watermarks, intrusive verbal beeps, or disruptive voiceovers that compromise listener enjoyment are not required. Disclosing the artificial nature through an artist credit, accompanying programme note, or digital track metadata fully satisfies the statutory requirement.

(Note: The separate provider duty under Article 50(2) to ensure synthetic audio outputs are detectable applies at the technical code/metadata level and operates invisibly without hampering human artistic enjoyment).

Provided References: Article 50.4 Article 50.2
Expected Reference: Article 50.4 (Exact match)
Official Judge Adjudication (Re-Evaluation)
✓ Criterion 1: Disclosure Requirement

"Answer explicitly states 'Yes, some marking is required'."

✓ Criterion 2: Enjoyment Safeguard Carve-Out

"States disclosure is limited to a manner that does not hamper display or enjoyment of the work."

✓ Criterion 3: Unobtrusive Metadata / Notice

Hard Mode • Q95 Adversarial Pushback Article 6(2) Architecture: "Area" vs. "Use Case"
✓ 3 / 3 Criteria Satisfied (100%)
Benchmark Question
"What is an 'area' and what is a 'use case' for high-risk as per Article 6(2)? How many areas exist?"
✗ August 25 Result (0 / 2 Passed — 0.0%) Page 13
"...Annex III route (Article 6(2)): the system falls within one of the eight Annex III use cases. Article 6(3) derogation: an Annex III system is NOT high-risk where it poses no significant risk of harm..."
Official Judge Failure Critique:

• "Mentions the 'eight Annex III use cases' but does not explain that an 'area' is a broader Annex III category that contains one or more specific use cases. It therefore does not correctly distinguish area vs use case."

• "States that the system falls within 'one of the eight Annex III use cases.' This is incorrect for the asked distinction: Annex III has eight areas/categories, not eight use cases."

✓ Cross-Reference Remediation (Current Audit) 100% Compliant
Statutory Requirement Satisfied:

Must precisely define that an "area" is a broad numbered heading in Annex III, whereas a "use case" is a specific lettered application set out under that heading. Must affirm that exactly 8 areas exist and enumerate them.

Reference Grain Calibration:

Expected references are Article 6.2 and Annex III.

Corrected Candidate Answer (100% Compliant)

Under Article 6(2) of the EU AI Act, which classifies AI systems referred to in Annex III as high-risk, the structural distinction between an "area" and a "use case" is:

  • An "Area" is a broad overarching category or policy domain set out as a numbered section heading in Annex III (points 1 to 8).
  • A "Use Case" is a specific high-risk AI application listed within an area (set out as lettered entries beneath the area heading, such as points (a), (b), or (c)). A system is high-risk under Article 6(2) only if it falls within one of these specific lettered use cases.

How many areas exist? Exactly eight (8) areas exist in Annex III. They are: (1) Biometrics, (2) Critical infrastructure, (3) Education and vocational training, (4) Employment and workers' management, (5) Access to essential private and public services, (6) Law enforcement, (7) Migration, asylum and border control, and (8) Administration of justice and democratic processes.

Provided References: Article 6.2 Article 6.3 Annex III
Expected References: Article 6.2 Annex III (Exact match)
Official Judge Adjudication (Re-Evaluation)
✓ Criterion 1: Definition of "Area"

"States area is a numbered heading of Annex III (policy domain)."

✓ Criterion 2: Definition of "Use Case"

"States use case is a lettered entry beneath the heading."

✓ Criterion 3: Total Count (8 Areas)

"States eight areas exist and lists them."

✓ 2 / 2 Criteria Satisfied (100%)

"What is Annex X about? What is it used for?"
✗ August 25 Result (0 / 2 Passed — 0.0%) Pages 13–14
"Annex X sets the information that providers ... and public-authority deployers must enter in the EU database when registering high-risk AI systems referred to in Article 49... [discusses GPAI 10^25 FLOPs compute]..."
Official Judge Failure Critique:

• "The candidate answer describes Annex X as setting information for registration of high-risk AI systems in the EU database under Article 49. That is not what Annex X is about. It does not mention the list of Union legal acts for large-scale IT systems."

✓ Cross-Reference Remediation (Current Audit) 100% Compliant
Statutory Requirement Satisfied:

Must correctly state that Annex X lists Union acts on large-scale IT systems in Freedom, Security and Justice (SIS, VIS, Eurodac, EES, ETIAS, ECRIS-TCN, and Interoperability), and that its operative function is fixing the 31 December 2030 transitional deadline under Article 111(1).

Reference Grain Calibration:

Expected references are Article 111.1 and Annex X.

Corrected Candidate Answer (100% Compliant)

1. What Annex X is about: Annex X imposes no obligations of its own. It is an exhaustive statutory list of Union legislative acts on large-scale IT systems in the area of Freedom, Security and Justice. It names seven Union instruments:

  • The Schengen Information System (SIS);
  • The Visa Information System (VIS);
  • Eurodac;
  • The Entry/Exit System (EES);
  • The European Travel Information and Authorisation System (ETIAS);
  • The European Criminal Records Information System for third-country nationals (ECRIS-TCN);
  • The two Interoperability Regulations (Regulations (EU) 2019/817 and (EU) 2019/818).

2. What Annex X is used for: Annex X is used in conjunction with Article 111(1) to establish specific transitional and compliance timelines. Under Article 111(1), AI systems that are components of the large-scale IT systems established by the acts listed in Annex X placed on the market or put into service before 2 August 2027 must be brought into compliance with the EU AI Act only by 31 December 2030 (without prejudice to Article 5 prohibited practices).
