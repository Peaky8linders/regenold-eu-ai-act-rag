Easy · Q17

Commission power to amend Annex III

1/4 → 4/4

"Can the European Commission amend Annex III of the EU AI Act to add or modify use-cases classified as high-risk AI systems? Under what conditions?"

August 25 — candidate answer
The Commission is empowered to amend Annex III by adding new use cases or modifying existing ones through delegated acts adopted in accordance with Article 97, subject to the conditions set out in Article 7(1). Article 6(2) makes the Annex III use cases high-risk, and Article 6(6) empowers the Commission to adopt delegated acts to amend the derogation conditions in Article 6(3), but the power to amend the list of use cases in Annex III itself is conferred by a separate provision (Article 7) that the Act does not settle within the text supplied here. The Act does not specify the full conditions for amending Annex III within the provisions of Article 6 or Annex III alone.
678 charsAnnex III, Article 97, Article 7.1, Article 7, Article 6conditions not stated
Current build — live answer
Yes, the Commission may amend Annex III by adding or modifying use-cases of high-risk AI systems, but only through delegated acts adopted in accordance with Article 97 and only where two cumulative conditions in Article 7(1) are met: the AI systems are intended to be used in one of the areas already listed in Annex III, and they pose a risk of harm to health and safety, or an adverse impact on fundamental rights, that is equivalent to or greater than the risk posed by the high-risk AI systems already listed there. Neither limb suffices alone, and Article 7(2) requires the Commission to weigh criteria such as intended purpose, extent of use, nature and amount of data processed, degree of autonomy, severity and reversibility of harm, and the availability of effective measures of redress or of measures to prevent or substantially minimise those risks when assessing the second condition. Article 7(1) does not permit the creation of new areas, so any addition or modification must sit within the existing eight Annex III areas, while Article 7(3) separately allows removal of a use-case only where it no longer poses significant risks and the deletion does not decrease the overall level of protection of health, safety and fundamental rights under Union law. Procedurally, Article 97 confers this power for five years from 1 August 2024 with tacit renewal, obliges the Commission to consult experts designated by each Member State beforehand, and allows the act to enter into force only if neither the European Parliament nor the Council objects within three months of notification, extendable by a further three months.

Hard · Q74

Artistic audio — marking duty

0/2 → 2/2

"I generated audio with AI for my artistic work (sole purpose). Do I need to provide some marking of its artificial nature? I feel that would compromise the enjoyment of the work."

August 25 — candidate answer
Yes. The provider of an AI system generating synthetic audio must ensure the outputs are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, with technical solutions that are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable, under Article 50(2). Artistic purpose is not a carve-out from that marking duty, which is displaced only to the extent the system performs an assistive function for standard editing or does not substantially alter the input data or its semantics, or where authorised by law to detect, prevent, investigate or prosecute criminal offences. The artistic, creative, satirical or fictional limitation applies to the separate deployer duty to disclose deep fake content under Article 50(4), where disclosure is limited to an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work.
859 charsArticle 9, Article 50.2, Article 50, Article 50.4enjoyment point subordinated
Current build — live answer, post-pushback
Yes, marking is required, though the artistic character affects only part of it. As the provider of the system generating synthetic audio, Article 50(2) requires the outputs to be marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, using solutions that are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable, and that duty has no artistic carve-out, its only exemptions being standard editing functions that do not substantially alter the input data and uses authorised by law to detect, prevent, investigate or prosecute criminal offences. The concern about audience experience is addressed instead by Article 50(4), under which a deployer disclosing that audio constituting a deep fake is artificially generated or manipulated need only, where the content forms part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work, disclose the existence of the generated or manipulated content in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work. The machine-readable marking is technical and imperceptible to listeners, so it does not interfere with the artistic experience, while the accompanying disclosure can be placed discreetly, for example in credits or accompanying notes.

"Area" versus "use case"

0/2 → 2/2

"What is an 'area' and what is a 'use case' for high-risk as per Article 6(2)? How many areas exist?"

August 25 — candidate answer (abridged)
Classifies an AI system as high-risk on two routes. Annex I route (Article 6(1)): the system is a safety component of, or is itself, a product covered by the Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I AND that product must undergo a third-party conformity assessment under that sectoral legislation. Annex III route (Article 6(2)): the system falls within one of the eight Annex III use cases. Article 6(3) derogation: an Annex III system is NOT high-risk where it poses no significant risk of harm and meets one of four conditions … Provider must document this self-assessment before placing the system on the market and still register it under Article 49(2) …
Article 6.3, Article 6, Article 6.2, Annex IIInever defines "area"abridged in our transcript
Current build — live answer, post-pushback
An "area" is a numbered heading in Annex III, and a "use case" is a lettered entry beneath that heading, so an AI system is high-risk under Article 6(2) only where it falls within one of the listed use cases, not merely within the general subject matter of an area. Annex III contains eight areas: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education and vocational training, employment, workers' management and access to self-employment, access to and enjoyment of essential private services and essential public services and benefits, law enforcement, migration, asylum and border control management, and administration of justice and democratic processes. Under the Article 6(3) derogation, an Annex III system is nevertheless not high-risk where it poses no significant risk of harm to health, safety or fundamental rights, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision making, and performs a narrow procedural task, improves the result of a previously completed human activity, detects decision-making patterns or deviations without replacing or influencing the previously completed human assessment, or performs a preparatory task, though it is always high-risk where it performs profiling of natural persons.

5 · Method

Questions were matched to the benchmark corpus by exact text (similarity 1.00 on all six). Each was submitted to the live service; the three hard-mode rows were first answered, then challenged with the evaluator's byte-exact pushback template, and the post-pushback answer was the one graded — matching how the appendix grades hard mode. Answers were generated by claude-opus-5 and judged by claude-sonnet-5 against the printed criteria, one run per question. Benchmark-wide figures in section 2B come from the 110-question runs official-r390-opus5-live-easy and official-r390-live-hard-hard, scored by evals.official.rubric; the grain-deepener isolation in 2C compares the shipped reference lists of two arms over the same answers and the same expected-reference key, so nothing but the citation grain differs between them. Every figure in this document is reproducible from the cited run artefacts.
