  • Corrected answers. Asked whether a delegated act can add a brand-new area to Annex III, the system now answers “No” (Article 7(1)), where it opened “Yes, but only within the limits Article 7 sets.” A clinical decision-support question now gets the full area headings and the precise medical-device route (Annex I, Section A, points 11 and 12).
  • Complete, readable answers. Prepared answers keep their verdict lead and statutory clauses, which a length limit used to cut, and lists read as sentences. In a test of 182 prepared answers, 30 changed (10 distinct texts); a separate AI reviewer rated 18 of the 30 more correct and none less, at some cost in length.

How this was checked

  • Answers. The ten questions were asked again on the production system on 29 October 2026, one at a time, as single-turn questions worded as in the review (the multi-turn mode was not re-run). Answers and citations are identical to those of 26 October, and to those of 23 October apart from one punctuation mark in Q17 (Case D). With the AI model switched off the system returned the same answers, so they are the prepared texts, not generated text.
  • Wording. Every marked passage appears word for word in the answer shown, and wording shown as removed is absent. The 87 statutory passages the answers rely on were found in the Official Journal text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and, for Case D, in Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (consolidated version of 19 July 2026). The answers restore the Act's wording where the review flagged abridgement; elsewhere they follow it closely or paraphrase it, and the notes record the differences found.
  • The review. The verdicts are the review's own; the 22 quotations attributed to it are word for word from the review document or the 29 September answers.
  • Rewordings. To test that the corrections do not depend on exact wording, 20 further rewordings (two per question) were asked on 26 October and graded by a separate AI reviewer against the review's corrected positions: 11 of 20 correct before the second round, 14 on 23 October and 20 on 26 October (10, 13 and 19 on hand check). They guided later fixes, so they show what was fixed, not independent evidence.

Open limitations

  • Unfamiliar rewordings still reach the AI model, not a prepared answer. 28 of the 34 new rewordings do (checked without the model), and 6 of the 20 follow-up ones (live). Prepared answers are found by recognising how a question is put, so they fit the phrasings they were tested on. On hand check, 5 of the 6 follow-up answers the model wrote were correct; the other reached the right conclusion on fatigue monitoring but made the physical-state ground depend on the system's purpose, which Recital 18 does not. One of the correct 5 cites a provision that does not fit (Article 26, for an AI spam filter).
  • A prepared answer keeps its opening when the question differs. “What procedure applies when the Commission wants to update the list of high-risk use cases?” receives the Article 7 answer, which opens “Yes, but only within the limits Article 7 sets.” The procedure that follows is accurate, though it does not spell out the notification step (Article 97(5)).
  • Scenario-style questions. A long question that opens with a company description (a start-up building an AI for robot-assisted biopsies) reaches the AI model, not the Case D answer.
  • Differences from the review's corrected answers. Case B's Article 25(1) sentence omits “under Article 16”; Q45 item (f) omits “a description of”; Q16 does not say the use is “allowed only on conditions”; Q74 does not say artistic purpose is no exception to the provider's duty; Q95 names areas 1, 6 and 7 without the Annex III qualifier, as the review's list does. Some answers add content (Article 13(1) in Q45, Article 7(3) in Q17), and Q45, Q17 and Q74 cite more provisions than the official appendix expects (2, 3 and 5 against 1).
