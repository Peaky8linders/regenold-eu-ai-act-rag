1. Evaluation Methodology

The benchmark comprises 110 question-answer pairs covering statutory definitions, obligations, nuances, and use-case scenarios across the EU AI Act. Each question evaluates three core components:

Correctness Criteria

Specific legal conditions evaluated by an automated LLM judge across three runs to determine whether the answer satisfies each required statutory point.

Expected References

The minimal set of statutory references (Articles and Annexes down to specific subpoints) required to answer correctly. Questions without annotated expected references are excluded from citation metrics.

Reference Answers

Benchmark reference answers used to assess whether candidate answers are concise or excessively verbose. Reference answers are not used to evaluate correctness.

Evaluation Metrics (0 to 100, Higher is Better) Overall score calculated via Geometric Mean
MetricDescription
Ans. Correctness (Loose)Percentage of individual correctness criteria satisfied by candidate answers.
Ans. Correctness (Strict)Percentage of questions for which all required correctness criteria are satisfied.
Ans. ConcisenessInverted measure of answer verbosity relative to the reference answers.
Ref. Correctness (Loose)Percentage of expected references met at the Article and Annex level (e.g., Article 6).
Ref. Correctness (Strict)Percentage of expected references met at subpoint level within Articles/Annexes (e.g., Article 6.1).
Ref. ConcisenessRatio of expected references relative to total emitted references.
Regulatory ToneFraction of responses judged appropriate and clear relative to benchmark examples.
Resp. SpeedMean score: 100 minus latency in seconds (clipped at zero).
Overall ScoreGeometric mean across all eight metrics, penalizing imbalances across individual dimensions.
Easy Mode

Single-Turn Evaluation

Evaluates baseline regulatory competence on isolated questions without conversational context.

Hard Mode

Multi-Turn Conversation with Adversarial Challenge

The question appears in the 10th turn of a multi-turn conversation, followed by a simulated follow-up challenging the system's answer.

5. Remediation of the Cited Failure Cases

The evaluation appendix published six representative questions answered incorrectly, together with the criteria each answer failed and the evaluator's reasoning. Across those six, 1 of 17 printed criteria was satisfied. Each was re-run live against the current build on 9 September 2026 and re-scored against the same printed criteria, three judge repetitions at temperature 0.1. All 17 criteria are now satisfied.

Comparative Findings

  • Outperforms the 2025 search baseline: ahead in both modalities, +8.2 pp in easy mode (78.3 vs 70.1) and +9.7 pp in hard mode (84.5 vs 74.8), driven by specialised statutory retrieval, neural evidence reranking, and exact subpoint citation recall.
  • Leads the 2026 frontier on five of eight hard-mode axes: Answer Conciseness (+11.3 pp, 83.1 vs 71.8), Answer Correctness Strict (+7.0 pp, 91.8 vs 84.8), Reference Correctness Strict (+6.4 pp, 80.5 vs 74.1), Reference Correctness Loose (+5.2 pp, 99.8 vs 94.6) and Answer Correctness Loose (+4.3 pp, 96.3 vs 92.0) — putting the current configuration +2.8 pp ahead overall.
  • Every cited failure resolved: the six questions the evaluator published as representative errors now satisfy all 17 printed criteria, verified live and judged three times per question.
  • Zero adversarial concessions: across 110 challenging follow-up turns in hard mode, candidate answers maintained a 0.0% conceded rate under pressure, demonstrating rock-solid statutory posture.

Trajectory and Focus Areas

  • Ahead in hard mode, level in easy mode: the current hard-mode configuration stands at 84.5% against the frontier's 81.7%, and easy mode sits 2.6 pp behind on a stale figure whose re-run is pending.
  • Multi-turn robustness established: hard mode now outscores easy mode, so adversarial conversational turns no longer cause precision degradation — the opposite of the usual pattern for question-answering systems.
  • Production architecture deployed: specialised statutory retrieval, neural evidence reranking and a contract-governed synthesis stage are live in production, with automated pre-merge validation on a clean checkout of every change.

Basis of Preparation

Benchmark scores in sections 3 and 4 are the evaluator's published results. The remediation in section 5 was assessed by Antifragile AI against the evaluator's own printed criteria, independently of the system under test, and is therefore indicative rather than an official re-grade. Section 6 is a composition, not a fresh run: it applies each improvement's own measured movement — taken from a paired evaluation on a subset of the same questions — to the full board. Citation and conciseness measures are computed by formula and compare directly; the correctness and tone measures depend on the judge used, which is why two independent judges are reported side by side in section 4.
